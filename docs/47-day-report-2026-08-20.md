# What the day established, and what it cost to learn

*2026-08-20. Written for the operator. Every number here was re-derived at least once; where a claim
was made and later withdrawn, the withdrawal is in the text rather than in a footnote.*

## 1. The headline: the parameters in the record are not the parameters that ran

`Idea.params` is a PROPOSAL. `params_style: "none"` means the engine applies nothing and the
Developer realises it by editing the repo — so a deviation is legitimate and expected. What is not
legitimate is that the record keeps the proposal and every reader treats it as fact.

Measured over every run on disk: **457 comparisons, 41 diverged (9.0%), 18 of them on nodes that
produced a metric.** The e5 champion at **0.793426** is recorded as batch 8192 / accum 2 / 15 epochs
and **ran batch 512 / accum 32 / 3 epochs**.

That record is what put 8192 into the v3 goal, and v3 died with three nodes and no metric.

**And the carrier is not what anyone assumed.** `rubertlite-dr-unified-v8`'s champion at 0.762048 has
`config.yaml` saying 8192 and `vectorsearch/train.py:31` saying **4096** — with the Developer's
reasoning inline: R-Drop's second forward pass makes 8192 OOM even on a 140 GB H200, so it halves the
batch and doubles accumulation, and *"Config is pydantic-mutable, so this is a train.py-only change
(no config.yaml edit) that leaves the completed `mine` stage reusable."* It deliberately did not
touch the document, to preserve stage reuse. **"The config file is the config" is measurably false.**

Two resolved configs on that node disagree along the same seam: the TRAIN stage resolved 4096/4, the
SCORE stage resolved 8192/2, because scoring re-reads the untouched document. Since 0.762048 is a
training result, the train stage's resolution is the one that describes it.

## 2. Ten healthy nodes were killed by a 4,000-character window

`runs/rubertlite-dense-retrieval` has 16 `no_metric` terminals. **Ten of those nodes later scored** —
0.8050 to 0.8662 against a run best of 0.8835 — because they were reset and the train stage came back
`reused`, i.e. the very checkpoint the check had condemned.

The stage check asks whether the loss is **unchanged from the first training step**, and it is asked
of the last 4,000 characters — evidence that structurally cannot contain the first training step.
Node 1's log holds 11,248 loss points falling 33.9 → 13.3; its last 4,000 characters hold **three
points, all reading 13.3**. A converged run and a frozen one are identical through that window.

Fixed by measuring the trajectory over the whole attempt log, putting it in the checker's prompt and
vetoing a refusal it contradicts. Acquit-only. On the corpus: reachable rows 6/16 → 15/16, and
**wrong kills 10 → 0**. The asymmetry was measured, not asserted: a wrong kill cost 1,570-4,344 stage
seconds with no repair, no retry and no refunded slot; a wrong keep costs the 65-67 s of scoring that
then catches it.

This also corrected me. I had claimed for most of the day that those sixteen terminals were really
`not_learning`. **Exactly one is.** Ten were not failures at all, four are genuine divergence, and the
sixteenth is a stage that printed nothing.

## 3. Failure classification now splits by ownership — and the number arrived last

The engine classifies only what it CAUSED, RAN or MEASURED: its own timeout, both watchdogs, its own
metric cross-read, the return code of a step it launched. Everything at the level of the experiment's
code — `crash`, `no_metric`, `check_failed` — goes to a diagnostician riding the EXISTING triage
call, at **+0 provider calls**, because that call was already an 8.82-call agent per failure.

The first measurement said the change was inert: the deterministic half scores 88/118 = 74.6%,
**identical answers on all 122 rows** to the classifier it replaced. Both deleted text rules decided
nothing here — the marker rule already scored 0/23 over the durable record, and the exit-code
signature was shadowed by a watchdog branch on every row.

Then the diagnostician was actually replayed:

| | accuracy | authenticated | read from the text |
|---|---|---|---|
| deterministic half alone | 74.6% | 90.5% | 65.8% |
| diagnostician, durable record | **82.2%** | 66.7% | **90.8%** |
| diagnostician, widened window | **86.4%** | 69.0% | **96.1%** |

**The win is entirely out-of-memory, and it beats the deleted rule on the rule's own ground**: no
preserved tail contains a marker, so `_is_torch_oom` scores 0/23 there; the model scores 18/22, and
22/22 with a wider window, by reading the allocator's message body.

Net: **+19 on readings, −10 on facts, +9 overall.** The loss is the ownership split's own price —
when the engine misfiles a row whose correct answer belongs to its half, the diagnostician cannot
rescue it, because that word is not in its vocabulary. On four rows it wrote the right diagnosis in
its rationale ("the true failure is the training objective diverging") and the wrong one in the field
everything keys on.

## 4. The number that explains why any of this was worth doing

Across the corpus, the engine is right **90.5%** of the time about what it OBSERVED ITSELF and
**65.8%** about what it READ FROM TEXT. Nine times in ten against two in three.

Accuracy alone hides everything that matters here: across the whole confidence sweep it moves only
0.678-0.706 while the count of healthy nodes killed goes 3 → 0. **The cost columns carry the signal;
the accuracy column does not.**

## 5. The stopper: what it sees and what it cannot

Classified over 450 real decisions. The judge is right when the failure is in the CURVE and blind
when it is anywhere else — **91% vs ~5%**:

| what happened | decisions | said "broken" |
|---|---|---|
| trained fine, metric ~0 | 53 | 48 — 91% |
| exited 0, engine failed it on a check | 23 | 1 |
| exited 0, engine failed it on artifacts | 15 | 1 |
| killed by signal | 41 | 2 |
| crashed with an exception | 22 | 1 |

Three different blindnesses: **nothing to see** (the training log records a successful training run,
because it was one), **the signal is silence** (a killed log stops mid-line, and a stopped log looks
exactly like one still being written), and **the evidence is in another file** (a traceback lives in
stderr while the judge reads the training log).

Its false alarms turned out to be a threshold, not a prompt: all five sit at confidence 0.62-0.75
while the engine acts at 0.80, so **the engine never acted on any of them** — 0 of 49 productive
attempts falsely stopped. The tools were not the fix either: 0.782 with them, 0.765 without, inside
the model's own noise. **No prompt was changed.**

Do we always stop a run whose loss exploded? **No.** `reason: diverged` has been a terminal **twice
in the history of eight runs**. The watchdog is armed only for a declared stage, needs five
consecutive non-finite records, and any finite record on either stream clears the count.

## 6. Nine mechanisms shipped a guard that could not fail

One shape, found nine times in one day: **a guard that names the MECHANISM rather than the
PROPERTY** passes without the behaviour. The open-item proof window inheriting a neighbour's
falsifier (17 of 77 markers). A claim checker printing "0 defects" over zero pins. The declared-params
guard reading `.py` while the carrier was YAML. Two skill pins a comment satisfies. A `max()` pin with
unconstrained operands. An ASHA control that could not tell "stayed quiet" from "never ran". A
`f(x) == f(x)` round-trip. A derivation guard that always skipped. And seven substring pins inside the
guard for the change that removes text-matching from the engine.

The rule that came out of it: **every assertion needs an input that makes it fail, and every count
needs to distinguish "all passed" from "nothing was checked".**

## 7. What I got wrong

- Claimed all sixteen `no_metric` terminals were `not_learning`. One is.
- Claimed the params guard "finds agreement" in `config.py`. It cannot match at all — a one-part path
  against a two-part declaration. Same empty answer, different mechanism.
- Invented a denominator: said the resolved config covers "27 of 99" scored nodes. It is 28 of 29,
  and what disqualifies it as primary is that 28 of 52 nodes hold more than one and 8 of those
  disagree.
- Escalated the lessons cadence to a defect on 24 repeated spans. The spans have no children — the
  gate returns before any work, and all 21 generations in that window belong to the training watchdog.
  I counted spans and inferred work, having earlier counted a cumulative total and inferred activity.
- Published a decomposition of ten lost rows built on a filter that conflated correct and incorrect
  ones. Withdrawn; the +19/−10/+9 split stands because the bench prints it, the composition does not.
- Six times committed from a checkout whose branch another agent had moved, once swept another
  agent's staged files into my commit, once untracked a file that had been on master for a week, and
  once deleted a live `index.lock`. Every one impossible in an isolated worktree — which I had given
  every other agent and not that one.
- Merged the diagnostician with `reason_evidence` persisted **without redaction** — the eighth
  model-authored output channel, sitting one line below `rationale`, which has passed `_redact` since
  B3 with a comment saying why. An agent found it, not me, and priced it: over 257 preserved logs a
  500-char window carries 0 secret masks, a 16 KB read carries **36 including a real `password`**, and
  64 KB carries 384 — and that role reads WITH TOOLS, so its quotable window is the whole file. The
  lesson generalises past this field: **widening what a model may quote widens the secret surface**,
  and any future widening has to be priced the same way.

## 7a. A live sequence I read wrong three times, and what it actually shows

Written first as "the 500-character window, demonstrated live" — **that claim was wrong and is
withdrawn**. The full sequence, corrected, because every wrong reading of it was mine and each was
made from real evidence.

`runs/e5small-dr-unified-v4` node 3, batch 8192 with gradient checkpointing:

* **attempt 1** — `NameError: name 'q' is not defined. Did you mean: 'q1'?` at 250 s. I checked the
  durable `error_in` and found only `accelerate`'s own frames: `simple_launcher`, `launch_command`,
  `CalledProcessError`, beginning mid-word at `i.py", line 50, in main`. Measured: the `NameError`
  sits **1,140 characters from EOF** in a 37,378-character log, and the window is 500. I concluded
  the repair was blind. **It was not.** The repair's own explanation names it exactly — *"loss.py:558
  references undefined `q`; should be `q1`"* — because the Developer has had `repair_log_tools` since
  2026-08-15 and read past the window. So the thin record did NOT defeat this repair, and the
  log-tools fix demonstrably works.
* **attempt 2** — fixed the sibling name; both `p` and `q` are defined on disk now.
* **attempt 3** — trained for real, peaked at **131,489 MiB of 143,771**, and died at 284 s on
  `torch.OutOfMemoryError: Tried to allocate 2.25 GiB. GPU 0 has a total capacity of 139.80 GiB of
  which 151.06 MiB is free.`

**On the memory prediction.** I predicted an OOM, and there was one — but my reasoning was wrong and
should not be credited: I argued that doubling the per-device batch would double activation memory to
roughly 200 GiB. The measurement is 102 GiB at 4096 and 131 GiB at 8192 — **+29 %, not +100 %** —
because with checkpointing the activations are not the dominant term. It died on a single 2.25 GiB
allocation against 151 MiB of headroom, not on the doubling I described.

**And on the guard I wrote to test it.** My monitor announced "the OOM prediction was WRONG, 8192
fits" at a 131 GiB peak, because I had given it the criterion `peak > 120 GiB → it fits`. That is a
guess dressed as a criterion. **A memory ceiling is not proven by a peak below it — it is proven by
the run finishing.** I built a guard whose success condition is reachable before the question is
settled, which is section 6's defect, committed by the author of section 6, on the same day.

**What the sequence actually shows, and it is not what I first wrote:** the repair loop worked. Each
attempt fixed the thing in front of it and exposed the next one — a typo, its twin, then a real
resource ceiling. What it cost is three attempts and ~13 minutes of GPU to arrive at a question
(does 8192 fit?) that node 1's own run had already answered in part, and that nothing records.

## 8. What is true and still unfixed

- The durable record keeps ~500 characters while the classifier reads 64 KB. Widening the evidence
  moves 16 rows. The diagnostician is handed exactly the rows whose durable evidence is thinnest.
- The comparability key is merged and was INERT until this evening, when the task finally declared
  its inputs. Nothing already on disk can be retro-fitted; those runs read `unknown` and were not
  guessed.
- The run cannot re-plan while it is busy: the creation-boundary consult is reachable only between
  nodes. Measured live — zero cadence events for 2.5 hours with both GPUs held, then four within one
  minute of a node terminating. On a task whose nodes train for nine hours, that window is most of
  the run.
- The diagnostician promotes the stage checker's PROSE to a cause — the same reading-as-evidence
  defect, one layer up, and the most expensive single population in its confusion matrix.
