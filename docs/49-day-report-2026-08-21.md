# The record stopped lying, and the watchdog's diagnosis finally reached the agent that repairs

*2026-08-21. Written for the operator. Every number here was measured on this box; where a claim was
made and later corrected, the correction is in the text rather than in a footnote.*

## 1. Two agents watched one run for ten hours and reached opposite conclusions

`runs/e5small-dr-unified-v4` node 3 drew **21 `train_monitor_alert` rows** over ten hours. Fifteen
named a mechanism and converged on it precisely: the DCL mask sentinel is a FINITE `-1e9`, so a row
whose mask removes every negative drags the batch mean by roughly `-1e9/batch` and the contrastive
objective — bounded below by 0 as written — becomes unbounded below. The measured trajectory on
those rows never wavered: `direction=descending`, `first=40.07`, `last=-2.4e7`.

The stage then hit its ceiling. The repair judge was handed the stderr tail, the code tail and the
repair history — **and not one word of any of that** — and opened: *"Healthy training run … a pure
speed failure, not a correctness one."* It cut `n_epochs` 15 → 3, left `dcl_threshold` and the
sentinel untouched, and the next attempt re-ran the same degenerate objective. **~17 GPU-hours.**

Nothing new is measured to fix this. The rows were already durable, already keyed to the node,
already paid for. The change is a READ: the verdicts are spliced into the repair prompt under a
heading that says **whose opinion it is**, each row carries its own confidence, and the series is
**not collapsed** — on this node the watchdog said `healthy` at 0.85 five times, twice directly
between two `broken` verdicts at the same confidence, and that it had no settled answer is
information the Developer should have rather than something an average hides. The `trajectory` rides
alongside on its own `measured:` line because it is the one field on those rows that is not a
judgement: the verdicts contradicted each other five times and the trajectories never did.

## 2. Why nothing stopped it — and why that is not a bug to fix

**Zero of node 3's 24 alerts carry `stop_decided` or `kill`.** Not "the bar was too high": five
`broken` verdicts sat at or above the 0.8 bar (0.85, 0.80, 0.85, 0.85, **0.90**), all attributing
the fault to the implementation. Each was blocked, and by two different rules:

| alert | confidence | streak before | direction | blocked by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| #5 | 0.85 | 0 | descending | streak < 2 **and** the trajectory veto |
| #8 | 0.80 | 1 | descending | streak < 2 **and** the trajectory veto |
| #11 | 0.85 | 0 | descending | streak < 2 **and** the trajectory veto |
| #16 | 0.85 | 0 | unknown | the streak alone |
| #18 | **0.90** | 2 | descending | **the trajectory veto alone** |

`trajectory_vetoes_kill` refuses to end a descending, non-anomalous curve. For a loss bounded below
that is right. Node 3's objective is unbounded below, so **descent is the symptom**. The explosion
rung that should have caught it reads `abs(window.maximum)` — the SIGNED max, i.e. the value nearest
zero for an all-negative loss — so it structurally cannot fire on a run diverging downward.

This is already a permanently declined item, with the arithmetic:
`DECLINED[explosion-rung-cannot-be-magnitude-symmetric]`. 28 of the 249 stage logs on this box reach
|loss| ≥ 1e8 and 26 of them produced a metric, 16 above 0.87. Any bar that catches the broken node
n74 (peak 2.54e+08) kills champion n48 (2.53e+08, metric 0.8835) — 5.64x versus 5.65x, and the
champion is higher. So the monitor is **structurally barred** from stopping this class of run, which
is exactly why getting its diagnosis to the repairer is the answer rather than a consolation.

**And the streak is defeated by a wobbling judge.** Node 3 drew 13 `broken` verdicts in 24 and never
two in a row at ≥0.8, because `healthy` verdicts at 0.85 interleave. The gate asks for CONSECUTIVE
agreement from a judge that is measurably not consistent.

A windowed rule — "at least 2 of the last 6 are broken + implementation + ≥0.8" — fires on **exactly
one node in 213 alerts across 30 nodes**: this one. It fires on **zero** nodes that produced a
healthy metric, including all four champions and `e5small-dr-unified-v2` node 2, which drew 33
alerts and scored 0.0. That is a JUDGE-shaped rung rather than a trajectory-shaped one, so the
declined item — whose corpus has **zero** watchdog rows — does not cover it. **It is not shipped:
n=1.** Perfect separation over 30 nodes with one positive case is evidence the rule does not fire
wrongly on 29 known cases, not evidence that it generalises. And the windowed change alone would be
inert, because the veto was #18's only remaining blocker.

## 3. The records that lied have been repaired

`Idea.params` is a PROPOSAL. Under `params_style: "none"` the engine applies nothing — the Developer
realises the idea by EDITING THE REPO — so a deviation is legitimate. What was not legitimate is
that the record kept only the proposal while every reader presented it as what produced the metric.

`looplab backfill-applied-params <run_root> --apply` went back and read the workdirs that survive.
**23 events written; a second run wrote 0.** Of 23 nodes with a metric and no record: **21
recovered, 2 not** (both declare no numeric coordinate at all), **6 diverge** from the proposal and
**6 have carriers that disagree with each other**.

* The v2 champion at **0.793426** is recorded 8192 / 2 / 15 and **ran 512 / 32 / 3**.
* The v8 champion at **0.762048** comes back as a **CONFLICT**: the config document says 8192 while
  the training script assigns 4096 — R-Drop's second forward pass will not fit at 8192 even on a
  140 GB H200, so the Developer halved the batch and deliberately left the document alone to keep
  the completed `mine` stage reusable. Both readings are kept, with file and line. Neither is
  declared to be "what the node used", because the record cannot say.

`champion_caveats.applied_params_diverged` already counted both divergences and conflicts; it simply
had nothing to read. It now fires on both champions.

Two properties make a record repair safe, and they are the whole design: **a live record always
wins** (a reconstruction may never overwrite a measurement, which is also the entire idempotence —
the second pass finds what the first wrote and declines), and **absence is recorded as absence** (a
node whose workdir is gone gets a row that says so, because "the workdir is gone" and "the proposal
is what ran" are opposite statements and the second is the one every reader made by default).

## 4. An agent can now ask what actually differs between two nodes

`diff_nodes(left, right, section?)` returns the experiment's own source diff, the parameters
PROPOSED and the parameters that actually RAN as **two separate columns**, and both metrics with the
comparability verdict attached. Read off the folded `RunState`, so the files shown are the ones the
node's LAST attempt ran — v8 node 3 was repaired five times and ended with seven files against the
five it was created with, and its metric belongs to the seven.

The property the tests are built around: **an empty diff and a missing diff never render the same.**

Driving it over the just-repaired record found two more real gaps: it rendered `diverged` and
**silently dropped `conflicts`**, so it printed nothing at all about the v8 champion; and it called
an UNSETTLED coordinate `(absent)`, the same absence-versus-emptiness confusion one level down.

## 5. The record now keeps 16 KB of a failure the classifier read 64 KB of

`res.stderr` was clamped at 64,000 bytes per stream when the classifier read it. 500 characters
survived to disk. **Not one of the 122 stored tails contains a torch-OOM marker** — five are a
launcher's opaque `Root Cause … exitcode: 1` block and two are nothing but a progress bar. The
deleted marker rule replayed over the durable record scores **0 of 23** OOMs and **16 of 23** over a
wider window; the diagnostician goes 82.2% → 86.4% on the same widening.

A SECOND column, not a bigger `error_in`: that one string is also the repair prompt, the judge's
history and the terminal's `error`, and widening it would multiply the paid prompt by 32. The guard
with teeth asserts **nothing on the prompt path reads the wide column**, and its positive half
asserts the judge's history still reads the narrow one, so "nobody reads either" cannot pass.

16 KB and not 64 KB, and the number is about secrets rather than bytes: the redactor fires 0 times
at 500 characters, **36** at 16 KB — catching a real `password` — and 384 at 64 KB.

## 6. The run was restarted, and why that was necessary

The engine launched 2026-08-20 03:22 and **pins its own code**. The dark-GPU fix merged 2026-08-20
18:52, 15.5 hours later, so it could never reach that process — GPU 1 was dark for 14.5 hours while
GPU 0 ran alone, and the event log (not a snapshot) is what says so: no stage has run on a second
node since node 1 finished.

`stop` was not enough. It breaks the engine on its NEXT loop iteration, which would have been ~5.6
hours away behind node 3's training. The process was terminated directly and resumed from its log,
onto master — recovering 27 commits including everything above.

**Measured inside v4, the one comparison genuinely on one scale:** node 0 ran 5 epochs in 4.72 h and
scored 0.758851; node 1 ran 15 epochs in 9.32 h and scored 0.764853. Tripling the epochs bought
**+0.006 for double the wall clock** — and both nodes DECLARE 8192 / 2 / 15 while node 0 actually
ran 4096 with 5 epochs and temperature 0.01.

## 7. What I got wrong

- **I merged and pushed on a suite I had not read the EXIT of.** `grep … | head && git merge && git
  push` — grep exits 0 when it MATCHES, so two `FAILED` lines merged and pushed anyway, and I read
  the merge commit's hash as confirmation. Master carried two failing guards until I noticed. Both
  guards were right: a new module must declare its layer, and `agents/factory.py` sat at 519 lines
  against a cap of 520, so *any* addition fails it. A cap already at its limit is the design saying
  the reasoning belongs in the module you are adding, not in the assembly that calls it.
- **`git checkout master` ran in a worktree instead of the main checkout**, and the first `resume`
  launched from a 27-commit-stale tree. Caught in 25 seconds; the process was killed before it wrote
  anything, and the log after `pause` is clean.
- **I filed "the diagnostician promotes the stage checker's PROSE to a cause, 8 of 10 rows".** It is
  5 of 22 on the durable arm, and reading those rows' rationales the diagnostician is mostly RIGHT
  and has nowhere to put it — *"The check_failed verdict is a false positive: the run actually
  reached val recall@100=0.8114, yet the verifier flagged…"*. The defect is a MISSING KIND, not a
  promotion.
- **I nearly filed a harness artifact as a product defect.** 4 of 122 bench rows carry `rationale:
  "placeholder"` beside a real action. Zero rows in any run's durable log do — it is the replay
  harness's own stub.
- **Four tests read live run directories and broke the moment the records were repaired.** Correct
  of them, wrong of me: the repair is a supported operation, and a suite that reddens because a
  record was legitimately repaired is measuring the directory rather than the code.
